# Co-branding, partner logos and approvals

Source: `01_LG_BI_Guidelines_V5_MASTER.pdf` Part 4, Brand Management, PDF p.216–233
(doc p.1–18 of that part).

This is the section that matters most for channel and retail work, because almost every
co-marketing piece with a dealer or a partner falls under it — and because getting a partner
lockup wrong is one of the few brand errors with contractual consequences, not just aesthetic
ones.

## Before the design question: is co-branding allowed at all?

The guideline names three cases where **the LG Electronics brand cannot be used with another
brand**:

1. LGE provides only limited components and cannot provide product warranty or customer service
   on the final product.
2. The final product does not meet LGE's required quality level, so using the brand would damage
   brand equity.
3. In light of social trends and norms, the final product or service could lead to negative
   consumer perception — gambling is the example given.

Before that, the business relationship has to be examined properly: management rights and exact
stakes, and business liability for production, sales, marketing, quality, distribution and
service. All divisions (H&A, HE, VS, BS) are responsible for the branding of LGE and its
partners.

**This is not a decision to make inside a design task.** If a piece pairs the LG logo with
another company's, the right move is to say that it needs Brand Management sign-off and to name
the rule, rather than to lay it out and hope.

## The four relationships, and which rules apply

| Relationship | What it is | Key rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Joint Venture / Affiliate** | a partnership entity | a template that ensures **equal balance** between logos; use the **full logo**, and **horizontal arrangements rather than stacked** where possible |
| **Joint Branding (Ingredient Branding)** | LG technology inside a third party's product | the **relationship statement must be placed on top of the LG logo** |
| **Joint Marketing** | a product or service co-developed by LGE and a third party | logos may appear together only if the mandatory conditions in the *Brand Usage Guidelines* are met; the relationship must be explicitly stated |
| **Co-Marketing for LGE and Sales Partners** | LG and a retail or business partner advertising or promoting together | see the spacing rules below — this is the channel case |

In every case where the two brands appear together on a co-developed product or service, **the
relationship between them must be explicitly stated** — the guideline's examples of the wording
are *Powered by / Provided by / Supported by / Designed by*.

## Co-Marketing with sales partners — the measurable rules (p.229)

Taking **X** as the height of the LG logo:

**Type A — a bar between the logos**

- When placing multiple logos side by side, **insert a bar taller than the logos** between them.
- The clear space on either side of that bar is **0.5X**.
- The partner logo sits at **X or 0.65X** — matched to LG's logo height, or slightly smaller.

**Type B — clear space instead of a bar**

- When placing multiple logos side by side, **maintain clear space between them equal to the
  size of the logos** — that is, a full **X** gap.
- The partner logo again sits at **X or 0.65X**.

**Three standing conditions**

1. Both companies' logos must be used **with a balance under mutual agreement**.
2. The branding of the two parties **must NOT be printed on the product together**.
3. All divisions and subsidiaries are responsible for co-developed product and service branding;
   contact Overseas Sales & Marketing Company for advice or review before final decisions.

## Joint Marketing lockups (p.226–228)

**With the symbol mark**

- *Vertical version* — the logos may be placed on top of each other.
- *Horizontal version, Type A* — when placing the logos in one line, **secure a minimum of the
  logo-sized space between the two logos**.
- *Horizontal version, Type B* — when placing them next to each other in one line, **a bar
  longer than the height of the logo must be placed between them**.

**Without the symbol mark** — the logotype may be used alone when it is referenced in one line
and the statement of relationship appears. In that case **the logotype must be LG Gray, Black,
or White** (white on a dark background). The partnership description phrase is placed in
combination with the logo.

**If the two logos look unbalanced, consult the GMG Brand Management team.** The guideline says
this twice, which is a fair signal that optical balance between two different logos is a
judgement call it does not expect a designer to make alone.

## Brand naming and the approval chain

Not a design rule, but it determines whether work can ship.

**Product and brand names** may be used only after approval. The chain (outside Korea):
proposal by the company (H&A, MC, HE, VS, BS) → Marketing Communication Division approval →
Brand Management Division of Overseas Sales & Marketing Company agrees → Head of GMG agrees →
Head of Company approves. Korea follows a parallel two-stage chain.

**Co-branding with a partner brand** requires a two-stage approval before launch, including
**prior consultation with GMG** and **prior approval of LG Corp**.

**Changing a product brand logo** — its shape, position or colour, or adding a sub-sub-brand
logo — runs through Design Lab, Brand Management and Identity Management to the Head of Business
Division.

The practical consequence for any deliverable: producing correct artwork is not the same as
being cleared to publish it. Where a piece introduces a new name, a modifier, or a partner
logo, say so and point at the approval route.

## Brand structure and modifiers (p.220–222)

Useful when naming things on a piece of collateral.

| Element | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Master Brand** | the representative brand, nurtured with sustainability, delivered as a priority, expandable across categories. **Must be marked on the product** — Symbol Mark + Logotype. |
| **Sub-Brand** | operable long-term (at least 2 years) with a set goal, philosophy and plan; exclusively ownable and trademark-protected. |
| **Category Brand** | represents specific LG categories or various products; recognised in the market and used long-term (at least 2 years). |

**Modifiers** note characteristics, specs and features, as a prefix or suffix. Three permitted
forms: numeric version sequence (I, II, III), size and volume (inch, litre), and others such as
Pro, Dual, New, Special, Season 2, Super, Deluxe, Mini, Prestige. A modifier **must always be
combined with a brand or product name and can never stand alone**.

Two named exceptions: applying product logos or sub-brand logos outside the master-brand
criteria needs additional approval; and *LG Business Solutions* is applied to B2B exhibitions,
marketing materials and websites, but products themselves carry the Master Brand in its original
form.

**Not specified in source (in this document):** the *Brand Usage Guidelines* and the co-branding
review checklist referenced here are separate documents that are not in the asset pack, so the
"mandatory conditions" for joint marketing cannot be checked from what is available. Say that
plainly rather than assuming a lockup qualifies.
