# Illustration, gestures, and Digital Logo Play

Two smaller asset families from the master guideline. Both matter mainly because an agent that
does not know they exist will invent something in their place.

## Illustration

Source: PDF p.110–114 (section 10, doc p.86–90).

Illustration adds *"a youthful and playful energy to our core brand, and warmth to key moments
in the experience."* Its role is to provide playful moments and encourage interaction from the
user, in a warm and playful way.

### Characters

LG has named brand characters, downloadable from LG's own asset portal:

- **LG Fashionista** — "a passionate fashionista and marketer, always confident in expression"
- **LG Gamer** — "a curious gamer & engineer, always eager to explore"

### Gestures

**Finger Heart** and **Hand Heart** are described as *symbolic brand elements that express the
warmth of our brand*. They are specific pieces of artwork, not a description of a pose to draw.

### Don'ts (p.114)

1. Don't use more than **one gesture per design**.
2. Don't use illustrations with LG gradients in a way that makes them illegible.

### Assets

**No illustration or character artwork ships with this skill.** These are download-gated inside
LG's systems. Do not draw a character, a Finger Heart, or an "LG-style" illustration to stand in
for them — a character is protected artwork on its own, and an approximation is a redraw. Ask
the user for the files, or design without illustration; nothing in the guideline requires it.

## Digital Logo Play

Moved to its own reference: **`digital-logo-play.md`**. The official assets arrived after this
file was written, so the "not shipped, go and ask" advice below no longer applies to it — the
16 transparent GIFs now ship in `assets/digital-logo-play/`, and the manifest there points at
the `.mov` masters. It still applies to everything else on this page.

## The general rule this page is really about

Two asset families the guideline treats as fixed artwork are still **not in this skill**: EI form
shapes and their After Effects templates (see `ei-form.md`), and illustration characters and
gestures. Digital Logo Play used to be a third; its assets have since arrived, which is a good
reminder that this list is a snapshot rather than a permanent state — check `assets/` before
telling a user something is missing. The pattern for what remains is the same:

1. Say plainly that the official asset is not available here.
2. Offer the route to get it — LG's asset portal, or a colleague on the brand or marketing team.
3. Offer a design that does not need it, since none of the three is mandatory.
4. Never approximate one and never describe an approximation as the real thing.

An invented EI form or a hand-drawn Finger Heart fails a brand review in exactly the same way a
redrawn logo does, and is harder to spot — which makes it worse, not better.
