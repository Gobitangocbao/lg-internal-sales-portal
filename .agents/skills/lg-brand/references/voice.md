# Voice — writing the words on the page

Source: `01_LG_BI_Guidelines_V5_MASTER.pdf` section 11, PDF p.123–130 (doc p.8–15).

A poster is words as well as layout, and the copy is the part a brand reviewer reads first.
Three principles, each with an explicit try-to / try-not-to list. The message examples are LG's
own published lines, which makes them the clearest signal of the register.

## 1. We write with a smile

**We try to**

- Use inclusive language — refer to ourselves and customers as **"we"**.
- Use descriptive and sensorial language.
- Lean on intelligent humour to bring warmth.
- Make it easily understandable by **the average 11-year-old**.
- Name products and technologies to clearly convey the benefits.

**We try NOT to**

- Refer to ourselves as "LG Electronics" exclusively.
- Write with too much of a functional manual tone, or overwhelm with complex technical language.
- Use slapstick humour.
- Portray people as silly or needing help.

**LG's own examples (p.124)**

> Our designers obsessed over the curves of these speakers,
> And drew lines for 4,208 hours. — *XBOOM360*

> You wish every side of your life beautiful, even the back of your TV. — *Objet Collection Posé*

> When you do it because you love it, Life's Good. — *InstaView® Range*

Note what these share: a specific number or a specific object, and warmth carried by observation
rather than adjectives.

## 2. We write with insight

**We try to**

- Point out life's details and make people smile.
- Write about real life, and real people.
- Link products and customers' lives clearly. The guideline gives a formula:
  **relevant SEO keywords + life benefits = a key sentence.**
- For ESG communication, include **tangible actions and measurable goals** as a must in core
  messages.

**We try NOT to**

- Make life seem overly perfect, or too boring.
- Always refer to the future.
- Excessively portray our products as heroes.
- Overly explain technical detail.
- Use vague language like "we make a sustainable tomorrow".

**LG's own examples (p.126)**

> These days, every hero needs a multiverse. So when you need a break from this world, step into another. — *UltraGear*

> Too much effort, too much worries can shake your confidence. Hustle light all your way, with your gram. — *gram*

> Nobody wants to listen to tumble dryers, so we did for 365 days. — *6 Motion Direct Drive*

## 3. We write with design

This is the one with hard formatting rules, and it is where most drafts go wrong.

**We try to**

- Design messages to fit the platform.
- Create rhythm with rhyme.
- **Headline: 5–8 words.** Sentence case except proper nouns. **No full stop at the end** —
  except when there are more than two phrases, where full stops divide the sentences.
- **Body copy:** sentence case except proper nouns.
- Clarify the distinct role of eyebrow, headline and body copy.

**We try NOT to**

- Use copy that is hard to read or not optimised for the platform.
- Use line breaks that disrupt visual flow.
- Overuse impersonal, formal language.
- Repeat the same message across eyebrow, headline and body copy.

**The worked correction (p.128)**

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| New Energy Labels on Fridge Freezers for Sustainable Living | Greener choice. Cooler planet. |
| What's to Love about LG Fridge Freezers? | Much to Adore. The Fridge with More. |

The critique attached to the "before" versions is precise: *too generic*, and *written in title
case*. The "after" versions are praised for *rhythm with rhyme* and *sentence case*.

**Full application examples (p.129–130)**

> Driving better future mobility — *LG Vehicle Component Solutions*

> See more details. Feel more inspired.

> We know IT / We innovate it

> Light up your business — *LED Signage*

> For a long-lasting planet, we design materials to last a lifetime

## Applying this outside English

The guideline is written in English and its rhythm-and-rhyme advice is language-specific. The
transferable rules are the structural ones: 5–8 words in a headline, sentence case, no trailing
full stop on a single-phrase headline, distinct roles for eyebrow / headline / body, and no
repetition between them.

For Vietnamese, sentence case is the natural register anyway, and the "understandable by an
11-year-old" test transfers directly. Rhyme rarely survives translation — reach for rhythm and
a concrete detail instead, which is what the English examples are actually doing underneath the
rhyme.

## A note on claims

The voice section is about tone, not accuracy. Any figure that reaches a headline — a capacity,
a cycle time, a dimension — has to come from an official product page or spec sheet, and the
skill's rule against inventing numbers applies to copy exactly as it applies to colour values.
"4,208 hours" works because LG could stand behind it.
